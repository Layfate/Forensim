# Forensim 3D assets

The source file is `forensim_assets.blend` (scene `Forensim_Assets`). The exported `.glb` files are in
`app/src/main/assets/models/`. Every model is built from custom meshes (boxes, lathed profiles,
extruded outlines and displaced grids). No sphere primitives are used.

## Conventions

- glTF 2.0 binary, Y-up, metres. App coordinates are used as-is: x = room width, z = room depth, y = up.
- **Furniture and evidence:** the origin is at the bottom centre, and the footprint matches the
  collision box in `CaseCatalog.kt` / `ScenarioTemplate.kt` exactly. Place each model at
  `(spec.x, 0, spec.z)` (furniture) or at the evidence AABB's bottom centre, with no rotation.
  Top surfaces sit at `h`, so furniture-top spawn points line up with the model.
- **Environments** (`<location>.glb`): the walls, floor, ceiling, windows, door and wall decor of
  each room. The origin is the room corner, so the interior spans `x 0..width`, `z 0..depth`,
  matching `SceneGenerator`. Collision still comes from the AABBs. Nothing in a room shell stands on
  the floor except flat rugs and floor markings, so floor spawn points stay clear.
- Front-facing pieces face into the room: wall units on the east wall face -x, the drawer chest
  (west wall) faces +x, and the scene entry log faces -z (it stands just inside the south door in
  every location). Everything else faces +z.
- Flat evidence (prints, stains, note, hair, glass, packet) starts 3 mm above its surface, and
  rugs and floor markings are at most 2.5 mm thick, so evidence never sinks into a rug.
- No two parts of a model share an overlapping face in the same plane. `fz_fix_coplanar.py`
  (a text block in the .blend) nudges the smaller part back by about 1 mm. Run it after rebuilding
  any model, then export with `fz_export.py`.

## Known differences from the collision boxes

- `desk_a` and `desk_b` include a monitor at the back-left, so they are 1.29 m tall where the box is 0.75 m.
  The spawn points on the desk tops are clear of the monitor.
- `partition` (apartment): its box (x 3.5..6.5) overlaps the bed's box (from x 6.4) by 0.1 m. The
  model stops 0.15 m short of the box's east end so it doesn't cut through the bed. The box is unchanged,
  so existing cases build the same scene. Moving the partition in `CaseCatalog.kt` would fix the
  data properly, but it would change evidence placement for cases that already exist.
- `firearm` lies flat on its side (0.13 x 0.032 x 0.21 m). `EvidenceType.FIREARM.size` is still the
  upright box `(0.06, 0.13, 0.21)`. Changing it to `(0.13f, 0.035f, 0.21f)` would make the collision box match the model.
- The `sofa_top` spawn point is at y 0.8, which is the sofa's collision-box height (the top of the
  backrest), at the sofa's centre. The seat cushions are about 0.5 m high, so an item there floats
  above the seat. To fix it, the spawn point has to move onto the seat (about y 0.5, z 2.35), and the
  generator then needs a support whose top is at 0.5, because `isValid` requires a furniture top
  within 2 cm of the spawn height.

## Where evidence goes

Since 2026-11 every room places evidence at random (layout version 4 for residence, apartment and
office, 3 for store and warehouse, 1 for the five new rooms; older cases keep their fixed spots).
The case seed picks a free spot anywhere on the floor or on one of the room's flat furniture tops
(`ScenarioTemplate.surfaces`, set in `CaseCatalog.kt`) and turns the item to a random angle. So a
furniture top that should hold items must be flat at its surface height: keep monitors, pillows,
sinks and hobs out of those areas (see the `Top(...)` entries), and keep bed duvets within a few
millimetres of the top.

## Rebuilding

The build scripts are kept inside the `.blend` as text blocks (`fz_lib.py`, `fz_furn_*.py`,
`fz_ev_*.py`, `fz_rooms*.py`, `fz_textures.py`) for reference. The 2026-11 additions are also in
`art/scripts/`: run `fz_bootstrap.py` first in a fresh session (it recreates the shared helper
namespace and the `M` material table), then `fz_furn_common.py` + `fz_furn_v2_a.py` / `fz_furn_v2_b.py`,
`fz_rooms_common.py` + `fz_rooms.py` + `fz_rooms_v2.py`, and `fz_ev_common.py` + `fz_ev_common2.py` +
`fz_ev_batch5.py`, all into `bpy.app.driver_namespace['fz_G']`. `fz_export.py` re-exports every
model into `app/src/main/assets/models/`. `GlbAssetsTest` checks that the exported models still
match the collision boxes.

## Victim bodies

`models/victim/victim_<male|female>_<face_up|face_down>.glb`: the victim's body as the professor
placed it, one model per sex and position (about 12k triangles and one 1024 px baked texture each).
They come from `art/scripts/fz_victim.py`, which builds the body procedurally in the lying pose:
lofted torso, head with face, hands with fingers, clothes, shoes and hair. It merges the parts with a
voxel remesh, adds cloth wrinkles, decimates the result (keeping more detail in the head and hands)
and bakes colour plus ambient occlusion (with the floor) into the texture. Male: blue shirt, belt,
dark trousers, black shoes, short hair. Female: red blouse, knee-length skirt, flats, long hair spread
on the floor. Face down, the head is turned to one side, the right arm is raised beside it and the
left knee is bent.

- The origin is the centre of the body on the floor and the head points to +x. Each model stays inside
  `VictimPlacer`'s collision box (half length = length / 2 + 0.06 m, half width 0.41 m); `GlbAssetsTest`
  checks this.
- Rebuild in Blender: `ns = {}; exec(open(r"<repo>/art/scripts/fz_victim.py").read(), ns);
  ns["build_all"](r"<repo>")`. It takes about a minute and writes the four `.glb` files (JPEG
  textures). PNG copies of the textures go to `art/textures/`.
  `art/forensim_victims.blend` holds the four low-poly results.
- Without the files, the renderer falls back to the old primitive body.

## In the app

`engine/renderer/Glb.kt` reads the `.glb` files (meshes, base colour textures, emission, alpha
mode). `GlesRenderer` draws them with the game's existing lighting, flashlight and fog, and draws
transparent parts last. `GameRenderer` draws the room, furniture, scene log, tape posts and evidence
from these models, and the victim's body from `models/victim` (also in the case builder's
`VictimPreviewRenderer`). `EvidenceViewerRenderer` shows the detailed evidence model. If a file is missing,
both fall back to the old primitive shapes.
